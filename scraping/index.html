01 / The brief

From browsing products

This project uses Node.js and Cheerio to automatically collect product information from 117 laptops. The resulting dataset provides a starting point for exploring prices, brands, product segments and possible demand signals.

02 / The approach

A website.
A script.
A clearer view.

The workflow turns product listings into information that is easier to compare and analyse.

01 — COLLECT

Bring the information together.

The Node.js script collects laptop information from Web Scraper’s e-commerce test site. Automating the collection avoids copying every product into a document by hand.

02 — STRUCTURE

Give every product the same format.

Cheerio is used to extract information from the page markup. Names, prices, ratings and other attributes are organised into consistent fields so products can be compared.

03 — GROUP

Make the assortment easier to read.

Products are organised into budget, mid-range and premium segments. This provides a way to explore the balance between different price positions within the dataset.

04 — INTERPRET

Ask what the numbers actually mean.

Prices and review counts can help identify questions worth exploring. They do not establish actual sales, profitability or customer demand on their own.

04 / Reading the results

An assortment with
a premium lean.

More than half sit in the premium segment.

The collected data lists 65 premium products out of 117. That is approximately 56% of the collected assortment.

This describes the composition of this test dataset. It does not show which segment sells the most or represent the laptop market as a whole.

05 / From data to decisions

Better questions.
More informed choices.

Where does the offer sit?

Price segments can help a marketer understand how an assortment is positioned. In a real business, this could inform which categories deserve a closer review before choosing a campaign focus.

What deserves attention?

Review counts and ratings can suggest products to investigate further. Product age and visibility may also affect those numbers, so transaction data would be needed to understand actual demand.

What would make this actionable?

A useful next step would be to connect product data with actual sales, stock availability and margins. That would make it possible to evaluate commercial opportunities beyond the listed price.
